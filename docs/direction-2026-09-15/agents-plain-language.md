# Karos agents — one page each

Every agent works the same way: it reads your profile, researches its platform, decides what is worth saying, checks it fits your direction, drafts it, and hands it to you for review. Nothing is posted without you. Every run makes the next one better: what the research found, what you said in feedback, and how the post performed all go back into your profile.

Every post we deliver comes with **the goal of the post**. Internally, every client has one written strategy: what we draft, when, and why. The strategy model (audience → their problems → funnel → goal per post → distribution → feedback loop) is built for LinkedIn first and adapted per platform on each page. The client chooses whether they see their strategy document.

**Cadence** is set by the client in their calendar, not per agent. Given that cadence, our sequencing decides which post goes when and why, from what performs and what is relevant now.

## LinkedIn (up and running) (owner [Shlomi Gueta](mailto:shlomi@karoslabs.com))

**What it does.** Writes one LinkedIn post per run for your company page or for one person on your team, in that person's voice, with the goal of the post stated. You publish it from LinkedIn in one click.

**What you give it.** Once: the company page details, the people who post (each with their profile), what is off-limits. Any time: what happened this week, what you want covered next. Per run: who it posts as, the kind of post, an optional note.

**How it works.**
1. First run builds the strategy from your profile: who the buyers are, the problems they must solve, the content map, the voice (a person's voice is learned from their real posts).
2. Every run starts by checking what shipped, what you did with it, and whether something more relevant has appeared.
3. Picks the next post from the map by the rotation: never the same stage or type twice in a row, promotional at most one in six, a timely post only on a real anchor.
4. Writes it. Every number has a source and a date. A regulated topic waits for the right framing. A customer story waits for consent.
5. If nothing is worth posting, it says so and ships nothing.

**Strategy.** Four parts: the right audience, the right content, the right distribution, a feedback loop.
- **Audience**: the buyer roles, then deeper: the 3 to 5 problems they actually have to solve. Content is built on the problems, not the titles.
- **Content = the funnel**. For every problem, three questions: can this get their attention, can it demonstrate our expertise, can it help them evaluate us as a vendor. Top: earn attention with the industry's open questions. Middle: show expertise by explaining why and how things work or fail. Bottom: help them decide with how to pilot, evaluate and measure.
- **Content map**: problem × stage → post ideas. That map is the topic pool; every row carries its stage.
- **Sequencing**: the client sets the cadence in the calendar; our sequencing decides which stage and type goes in each slot, from what performs and what is relevant now.
- **Distribution**: founder-led content goes out from the person's profile, the company page amplifies it. Each post carries a suggested day so the cadence stays steady; the source link sits in the first comment; the person replies to comments in the first hour.
- **Feedback loop**: every post gets an outcome: posted, posted with edits, skipped, change requested. Edits teach the voice, skips teach the topics, performance teaches the mix. Each run starts by reading what shipped and what you did with it, and adjusts the plan.

**What you get.** The post, its **goal** (earn attention / show expertise / help them decide), **who it is for** (the problem it speaks to), **why now** (news, trend, your request, or a post of this kind that performed), a suggested day, the first-comment link, and one click to open LinkedIn with the text ready. Your feedback becomes a standing instruction.

## Reddit (up and running) (owner [Shlomi Gueta](mailto:shlomi@karoslabs.com))

**What it does.** Finds live threads where your buyers are asking questions and drafts a reply in your account's voice. You post it yourself, from your own account. Always. Reddit bans automated marketing, so nothing is ever posted for you.

**What you give it.** Once: the account it drafts as, an honest picture of that account's history, communities that are off-limits, how you disclose who you are. Per run: an optional note, and how many threads to aim for (one to three).

**How it works.**
1. First run maps your corner of Reddit and reads each community's rules, with the date.
2. Each run finds one to three live, active threads worth answering.
3. Safety first: can this account post there, is a product mention allowed, is the thread alive.
4. Drafts two different replies per thread. Value first: the reply must help even with every product mention deleted. No AI tells.
5. Checks culture fit, that every claim is real, and that the reply adds something the other comments do not.

**Strategy.** Reddit is not a broadcast channel; it is answering. The funnel is the buyer's question, not our post.
- **Communities in three rings**: about you, about your category, and where buyers describe the problem without knowing your category exists. The third ring is the most valuable and the least obvious.
- **Question pool**: the questions that keep coming back, with real threads proving they repeat. Most sit in the middle and bottom of the funnel by nature.
- **Mode**: warming (no product mentions anywhere) until the account has earned it; then mentions only where the community allows it, with disclosure.
- **Cadence**: one thread per run, at most five a week.
- **Feedback loop**: which reply you took, what you edited, why you skipped. Two promotional flags in one community → value-only there. A removal → that pattern never again there.

**What you get.** Per thread: the link, **why this thread** (the question it answers, the ring, what the other comments miss), two replies to choose from, the disclosure line, and any warning (rewrite required, account too new). Copy, open the thread, paste, post.

## Instagram (beta) (owner [Tomer Erel](mailto:tomer@karoslabs.com))

**What it does.** Makes one ready-to-post carousel (4 to 8 slides) or a single image with a deep caption, in your brand, from your own photos or sourced imagery. Also usable for TikTok.

**What you give it.** Per run: what the post should be about, the channel, the format, the audience, anything to include or avoid, and your own photos or references if you have any. Your brand kit (colours, fonts, logo) comes from your profile.

**How it works.**
1. Reads your profile, your brand kit and what you have already posted.
2. Researches hot topics in your market and what performs on the platform.
3. Picks the topic and writes the copy slide by slide: the hook on slide one, the story across the rest, the caption.
4. Finds the images: yours first, then sourced or generated.
5. Renders the slides in your typography and colours, layered without overlap, and checks the result visually.

**Strategy.** The funnel applies, with formats doing the work titles do on LinkedIn.
- **Audience**: who follows, saves and shares in this niche, and what they save.
- **Stages**: attention = relatable or contrarian hooks, trends; expertise = educational carousels, how-to, teardown; decide = proof, results, offer.
- **Content system**: 3 to 5 on-brand post templates (a series per content type) so the feed reads as one system.
- **Format and visuals**: carousel or single image chosen per post by what performs and what fits the topic. No fixed ratio, no fixed rotation.
- **What already works**: your own top posts and competitors' top posts set the starting direction.
- **Feedback loop**: approve or change, plus saves, shares and reach per template → double down on the template that wins.

**What you get.** Every slide, the caption, the **goal** and content type, and the reasoning behind the post. Approve, schedule, or say what to change.

**Coming.** Seeing the topic first and approving it before the visuals are made, so we don't waste visuals the client doesn't want. A switch: autopilot, or let the user pick. On a manual run the client picks the topic first. When the run comes from the calendar the client is not active, so there it defaults to auto.

- **Cadence**: set by the client in the calendar; the sequencing decides which clip goes when.

## Newsletter (coming soon) (owner Albert Kattan)

**What it does.** Prepares one newsletter issue per run, written in your voice and rendered as an email. You send it from your own email platform. We never send.

**What you give it.** Once: your send day, your email platform, who the reader is, phrases you never print, any open compliance question. Per run: the theme of the issue, stories or links to include, the call to action.

**How it works.**
1. First run learns your voice from your own past issues, builds a pool of topics and a watch-list for your niche.
2. Each run scans the last seven days, picks from the pool, plans the edition, writes it.
3. A compliance check refuses the whole issue if a banned phrase or claim slips in. It never quietly rewrites around a rule.
4. Keeps the issue numbering so a subscriber never receives the same number twice.

**Strategy.** Subscribers are already in the middle of the funnel; the job is to keep them and move them.
- **Pillars**: 3 to 4 editorial pillars from your profile; every issue has one lead and short briefs.
- **Goal per issue**: educate, update, or convert. The mix follows the pillars and what readers open.
- **Pool**: the topic runway; an empty pool holds the issue rather than improvising.
- **Feedback loop**: sent, sent with edits, held and why; opens and clicks when you share them.

**What you get.** The email in dark and light versions, a plain-text version, the issue's **goal**, and a note that starts with anything you must confirm before sending.
