## 1. The template

**0. Header** — client · platform · seat · version · last changed by what (setup / feedback / performance / review).

**1. Audience sheet** — one row per role: role → problem they must solve → what must be true before they act → where they are online for it.

**2. Problems ranked** — the 3–5 problems ordered by roles sharing it, how directly the product solves it, how loudly this platform discusses it now; reason on the line.

**3. Content map** — per problem × stage (attention / expertise / decide): 3 ideas as `idea · goal (earn attention / show expertise / help them decide) · type · source needed · used on`.

**4. Voice card summary** — what the person sounds like · two phrases they use · two they never use · their stance on the category · the proof they can cite.

**5. Distribution plan** — who posts, per stage (person or page) · where · first-hour actions (who replies, by when; whose posts get comments before and after) · amplification (named employees repost with a line) · first-comment rule (link and source in the first comment, never the body).

**6. Sequencing inputs** — cadence: slots per week from the client's calendar · no-repeat: never the same stage, type or format twice running; subject window per platform · promo ratio: product-led at most 1 in 6 · timely post only on a real anchor (news, launch, client request, a performing post) · weight towards what performs.

**7. Feedback loop** — per post: outcome (posted / with edits / skipped / change requested) · original vs final text · reason in the client's words · goal, problem, stage, type, why-now as delivered. What changes what: edit → voice card; skip with reason → never-topic or standing instruction; "like" → weight of that type × stage; performance outlier → the mix.

**8. Measures per stage** — attention: impressions, new followers, reach beyond followers · expertise: comments, saves, completion, profile visits · decide: link clicks, DMs naming the post, replies from named buyer roles.

**9. Industry overlay** — claims needing a source · off-limits topics · competitors never named · the buyer's calendar (budget cycle, events) · language and region.

**10. Review cadence** — every run reads §3, §6, §7 · monthly rewrites the §6 mix and §2 rank from §8 · quarterly rebuilds §1 and §3 from the refreshed profile; the header logs the diff.

## 2. Why each section exists (evidence)

- **Audience and ranking.** MKT1's 30% juice rule: product at most 30% of the time, problems the other 70%, with 3–5 perceptions to own https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/mkt1-field-guide-to-b2b-startup-marketing-part-2 (2025-08-26); per-item roadmap fields are ICP, funnel stage, impact, effort, plus anchor content per stage https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/content-roadmap (2022-08-16).
- **Content map.** Welsh fixes the outcome first, then crosses themes with eight styles (observational, actionable, motivational, analytical, contrarian, X vs Y, present vs future, listicle) https://www.natashamusa.com/how-to-systemise-your-content-creation-using-justin-welshs-content-matrix/ (2025-03-17). Kramer runs research → newsletter → one post per finding, comments surfacing the next questions https://captureflow.ai/playbooks/emily-kramer (2026-07).
- **Voice card.** The analysed creators share the mechanic: thesis in line one, every claim tied to a number; Welsh's sponsored posts ran 60–80% below his average, so off-voice is measurable https://captureflow.ai/playbooks/justin-welsh (2026-07).
- **Distribution.** Personal profiles out-reach company pages by 561%; employee advocacy drives 30% of company engagement https://www.writtenlyhub.com/news/linkedin-engagement-down-50-algorithm-insights-report-2025 (2026-08-22). The first 90 minutes decide distribution https://mercermackay.com/thinking/blog/a-leaders-guide-to-the-linkedin-algorithm-what-the-data-says/ (2025); quality comments inside the first hour lift visibility 35% https://www.agorapulse.com/blog/linkedin/linkedin-algorithm-2025/ (2025-09-03). A comment counts twice a like, reply-to-reply threads reach up to 2.4×, one save equals five likes, links go in the first comment or lose the preview https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm (2026-08-11). Alić stays in the comments for the hour after publishing (67% comment-to-reaction) https://captureflow.ai/playbooks/jasmin-alic (2026-07). Employee-shared content gets 8× the engagement and 24× the reshares of brand channels https://everyonesocial.com/blog/employee-advocacy-statistics/ (2023-01-10).
- **Sequencing.** 2–3 posts a week in varied formats lifts visibility up to 120%; same format back-to-back is suppressed up to 20% https://mercermackay.com/thinking/blog/a-leaders-guide-to-the-linkedin-algorithm-what-the-data-says/ (2025); 3–5 a week, never more than one a day https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm (2026-08-11). The creators post 3.3–7.5 times a week, rarely at weekends; Acosta ends every post with a question and a repost ask https://captureflow.ai/playbooks/lara-acosta (2026-07).
- **Feedback loop.** Ahrefs ends every audit row in keep / update / merge / delete: a log must end in an action https://ahrefs.com/blog/content-audit/ (2023-09-15). Kramer tests one initiative at a time so change is attributable https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/mkt1-content-strategy-part-1 (2025-05-06). AuthoredUp's per-post set: impressions, reactions, comments, shares, CTR, compared post against post https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-analytics (2026-06-16).
- **Measures.** Page engagement by format: document 7.0%, video 6.0%, image 5.3%, text 4.5%, link 3.25% https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/linkedin-benchmarks/ (2026-03-16). LinkedIn "is not designed for virality" https://blog.hootsuite.com/how-the-linkedin-algorithm-works-hacks/ (2026-07-14), so decide is measured in clicks, DMs and named-role replies.
- **Review cadence.** Views −50%, engagement −25%, follower growth −59% year on year https://www.writtenlyhub.com/news/linkedin-engagement-down-50-algorithm-insights-report-2025 (2026-08-22): re-read monthly.

## 3. Platform adaptations

**LinkedIn.** As written; §8 adds saves and document completion.

**X.** §5 is a conversation roster: 20–40 accounts the buyers follow, replies and quotes daily as the attention stage. The open-sourced ranker weights a reply 13.5 and an author-engaged reply 75, against 1.0 for a repost, 0.5 for a like, −74 for negative feedback https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm-ml/blob/main/projects/home/recap/README.md (2023-04-05). Originals keep the calendar; the conversation layer has no slot.

**Reddit.** §1 is the three rings (about the client, about the category, where buyers describe the problem without knowing the category), each with rules and the date read. §3's funnel is the buyer's question: questions × ring, mostly expertise and decide. §5 is comment-first: 6 primary and 7 secondary subreddits, 90/10, crawl-walk-run https://sproutsocial.com/insights/reddit-marketing/ (2026-01-23); human accounts with karma outrank brand accounts, and Reddit is the #2 US SEO-traffic domain https://ahrefs.com/blog/reddit-seo/ (2026-07-01). §6 keeps the 30-day window; §8 adds upvote ratio and whether the thread ranks. Draft-only stays hard.

**Instagram.** §3 is formats × templates, 3–5 series so the grid reads as one system. Reels rank on reshares and watch-through, Explore on likes, saves, shares https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained (2023-05-31); §8 is sends, saves, completion per template; §6 picks format by performance, never a ratio.

**TikTok.** §3 is one series per stage; completion is the heaviest signal and follower count is not a direct factor https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you (2020-06-18).

**Newsletter.** §3 is 3–4 pillars × one goal per issue (educate / update / convert). Baselines 35.63% open, 2.62% click, 0.22% unsubscribe https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-marketing-benchmarks/ (2023-12). §7 records opens, clicks, replies against the issue's goal.

**Blog.** §3 is clusters by intent: informational = attention; comparison, how-to = expertise; evaluation, pricing, alternatives = decide https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-intent/ (2024-10-11). One pillar, 20–30 linked cluster pages https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/topic-clusters-seo (2026-06-17); measure rankings and AI-answer visibility per cluster https://www.semrush.com/blog/topic-clusters/ (2026-09-07). Cadence is per cluster.

## 4. Worked example

Client: Ledgerline (fictional), AP automation for mid-market finance teams · LinkedIn · seat: CEO.
1. CFO → 12-day close → needs proof automation cuts it without losing control → CFO Dive, r/FPandA.
1. Controller → approvals stuck in email → needs an audit trail auditors accept → finance groups, Big 4 alumni.
1. IT lead → one more integration → needs ERP fit and SOC 2 → comparison pages, G2.
2. Rank: close time (all roles, discussed weekly) · approval trail · integration risk.
3. Close × attention: "What a 12-day close costs a 200-person company" · earn attention · point of view · internal data.
3. Close × expertise: "Why AP automation stalls at the approval step" · show expertise · explainer · anonymised customer.
3. Close × decide: "How to run a 30-day AP pilot and what to measure" · help them decide · how-to · pilot checklist.
4. Plain, numbers first; says "close", never "digital transformation"; stance: automation is a control, not a shortcut; proof: 40 customers, 9-day median close.
5. CEO posts; page reposts next morning; CEO replies within 60 min; two named employees comment; links in first comment.
6. Tue/Wed/Thu; no stage or format twice running; product ≤1 in 6; timely only on quarter close or a regulation change.
7. Skip on "pricing" → never-topic; edit removing jargon → voice card line.
8. Attention: impressions, follows · expertise: comments, saves · decide: checklist clicks, CFO DMs.
9. No ROI claim without a named customer; competitors unnamed; nothing in the last week of a quarter.
10. Monthly mix review; quarterly rebuild after the intel regenerate.

## 5. How it fits the Karos flow

- **Built at setup**: target audience → §1–2; market strategy and competitor analysis → §2 rank, §9; product information → §3 decide rows, §4 proof; brand voice and branding guidelines → §4; intel report → problems in the buyer's words for §3.
- **Read at every run** in step 5 with the subject table, feedback log and what-works summary; step 7 picks from §3 under §6; step 9 stamps goal, for, why-now on the deliverable and the subject row.
- **Updated by** step 10 (edits → §4, skips → never-topics, likes → weights) and by performance ingestion (§8 outliers → §6 mix, §2 rank). Never by the client editing the funnel.
- **Two questions to the client**: (1) "Of these problems, which one do your best customers hire you for first?" (confirms §2). (2) "Who posts from their own profile, and who can reply in the first hour?" (fills §5 and the seat).
- **Never ask** what the six documents already answer, what to post about, how the funnel works, for metrics they do not have, or anything phrased as review, approval or sign-off.

## 6. Feedback for the developer

- `stage` is missing on the topic catalog and the delivered post; §3 is the catalog, so a row needs `problem`, `stage`, `goal`, `type`, `sourceNeeded`, `usedAt`.
- §7 is the feedback-log row plus the diff: store `originalText` and `finalText`; the voice lesson is the diff.
- §5 needs `amplifiers[]` and `firstHourOwner` on the client × platform record.
- Expose `subjectWindowDays` (Reddit 30) and `promoEveryN` (default 6) as per-platform settings rather than constants.
- Default mix until Albert decides: 3 attention : 2 expertise : 1 decide per six posts (decide posts need proof the profile may not yet hold); log it as a default.
- Seed §8 baselines from Socialinsider's per-format rates so month one reports against a baseline before the client has history.
